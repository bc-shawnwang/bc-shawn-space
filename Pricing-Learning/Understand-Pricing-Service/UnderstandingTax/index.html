Understanding tax WIP

1. Where tax fits

The one rule: tax never changes the price the merchant entered. It adds an inclusive amount and an exclusive amount next to it. Everything before tax (catalog price, price list record, rules, discounts, picklists, rounding) works on the entered amount.

One price line through the Tax RPC. Loops every 8s.

InEntered priceafter rounding
Step 1Entered asinclusive or exclusive
Step 2Shopper's zonecountry, group, customer
Step 3Store tax outonly some inclusive prices
Step 4Apply ratesby tax class and priority
OutInc and exnext to the entered amount
  • Which prices go through tax. In the estimate RPC, every price line of every item: calculatedPrice, price, salePrice, retailPrice, minimumAdvertisedPrice, both price range ends and each monetary bulk tier. Each line is taxed on its own, using the item's tax class.
  • What comes back. { as_entered, inc_tax, ex_tax } per line. saved is computed afterwards from these.
  • The cart is different. GetCartPricing does no tax. bcapp runs its own tax estimate on the cart after pricing. See section 8.

2. How tax changes the data

In the estimate RPC, tax turns every price field into a set of three amounts. The input is one named price line per field. The output keeps the same fields, each as { as_entered, tax_inclusive, tax_exclusive } (TaxPriceProto).

Price fields through tax (10% rate, example amounts)
The amber amount is the one that equals as_entered.
  • Input. One line per price that exists: calculatedPrice, price, salePrice, retailPrice, minimumAdvertisedPrice, both ends of priceRange and retailPriceRange, and each monetary bulk tier. A price that is not there is not sent. All lines of an item share one tax class and one "entered as".
  • Output. Each line comes back as three amounts. as_entered is the input, unchanged. One of the other two always equals it: tax_inclusive when the price was entered inclusive, tax_exclusive when it was entered exclusive. The other side is calculated.
  • Ranges and tiers. A range has a minimum and a maximum, each with its own three amounts. A bulk tier keeps its own amount and gets the three amounts as tax_discount_amount, only when the tier is a monetary amount. A percentage tier is not taxed and has none.
  • saved is not an input. It is computed after tax as retail minus calculated, once for each of the three amounts, and only when retail is greater than calculated.
  • No tax result. If the tax call fails or returns nothing for a line, that line comes back with all three amounts equal to the entered amount.
  • One exception to "one equals as entered". When the store's own tax is removed first (section 7), the two calculated amounts are worked out from the taxable amount, so neither need equal as_entered.

3. Entered as: global, inclusive or exclusive

"Entered as" says whether the number the merchant typed already contains tax. It decides which way the calculation runs: from exclusive up to inclusive, or from inclusive down to exclusive.

  • Where the setting lives. On the price list: use global, entered inclusive or entered exclusive. With no price list (mode None, or Discounts), the store's catalog setting applies.
  • Where it is resolved in the estimate RPC. TaxApplicationService turns each item's value into inclusive or exclusive: an explicit value stays, and global becomes the store setting. It fetches the store setting only if some item needs it.
  • What else it changes. It also changes what happens when a variant has no record, and whether a list can be layered. The full comparison is in Understanding PriceList, section 1.

4. Finding the shopper's tax zone

Rates belong to tax zones. First the service decides which zone the shopper is in (TaxZoneDeterminationService.getZoneId). The first matching case wins:

#WhenZone comes from
1The store's address target is merchant originThe store's default shipping origin address: country, subdivision, postal code (fetchMerchantOriginTaxZoneTarget).
2No customer ID, and the target country is unknown or geolocation for guests is offThe store's guest shopper tax zone setting.
3A customer ID, and the customer has a stored address of the configured typeThat address: country, subdivision, postal code.
4Geolocation for guests is on and the request's country is known, or a customer has no stored addressThe zone of the request's own target (its country). A customer with no address, and geolocation off or the country unknown, gets the guest shopper zone.
5No zone matches the lookupThe store's rest of the world zone. If that zone does not exist, the call returns an error.
  • What the estimate RPC sends. country_code and customer_group_id, plus customer_id when given. Subdivision and postal code are sent as none, so a zone that depends on them cannot match from this RPC alone.
  • Which address. The customer's billing or shipping address is chosen by the store's tax calculation address setting (taxCalculationAddressTarget).
  • Cached. Zone lookups use tax-zone-cache and rest-of-the-world-tax-zone-cache (Lettuce).

5. Tax rates and priority

Once the zone is known, the service loads the zone's rates for the item's tax class (getBulkTaxRatesGroupedByClassIdAndPriority). Rates have a priority. This is what makes compound tax possible.

SetupHow it is calculated
One rateA single tax on the amount.
Several rates, same priorityEach rate is calculated on the same base. Nothing compounds.
Several prioritiesPriorities run in order. After each priority, its tax is added to the base (exclusive) or taken out of it (inclusive), and the next priority starts from that new base. So a later rate is charged on the earlier tax too.

Rates are cached in tax-rate-cache. An item whose tax class has no rates in the zone gets no tax: inclusive equals exclusive equals as entered.

    7. Removing the store's own tax

    This is a setting, shouldSubtractStoreTax. It exists for stores whose prices are entered inclusive of the store's own tax and that sell to shoppers in other zones. Those shoppers should not pay the store's tax. It applies only when all three hold:

    • the setting is on,
    • the price is entered inclusive,
    • the shopper's zone is not the store's zone.

    Then, for that price line:

    1. The store's rates for the item's tax class are loaded (storeTaxRates). If there are none, nothing is subtracted, but the amount is still treated as exclusive from then on.
    2. The store tax is calculated on the entered inclusive amount with full precision and subtracted, which leaves an exclusive amount.
    3. The shopper's rates are applied to that exclusive amount, also with full precision (no rounding).

    Example, from the calculator: 120.00 entered inclusive, store rate 20%, shopper rate 10%. Store tax is 20.00, so the taxable amount is 100.00. The shopper's tax is 10.00. The response has exclusive 100.00 and inclusive 110.00.

    9. What the shopper sees

    Calculating inclusive and exclusive amounts is separate from choosing which one to show. Display is decided in bcapp, per tax zone.

    • Storefront. TaxPriceDisplaySettingsService finds the shopper's zone and reads its price display settings: show inclusive or exclusive by default, and whether to show both on product detail and list pages.
    • Invoices and the control panel. Each has a strategy: always inclusive, always exclusive, or follow the zone. A fixed strategy overrides the zone.
    • In the Checkout API. displayTaxInclusive is that zone result for the cart. salePrice reads the unit price on that side. comparisonPrice shows a strike-through only when there is a discount and the entered side equals the display side. See the field tables in Understand Client Side.
    • Discounts. With TAX-2513.convert_discount_amounts_to_tax_display_treatment on, discount amounts are converted to the display side.
